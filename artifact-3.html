The Artifact: A Two-Minute Explanation

How a Neural Network Learns (No Math Required)

Imagine teaching a kid to recognize dogs. You don't hand them a rulebook that says "a dog has four legs, fur, and a tail" — they'd call a cat a dog too. Instead, you show them lots of dogs. "That's a dog. That's a dog. Nope, that one's a cat." Over time, something in their brain quietly adjusts, and one day they point at a breed they've never seen and say "dog!" — and they're right.

A neural network learns the exact same way, in three small bites:

  • Bite 1 — It's a guessing machine. A neural network is a program made of thousands of tiny connected "switches" (loosely inspired by brain neurons). Show it something — a photo, a sentence, a sound — and it makes a guess.
  • Bite 2 — It learns from being wrong. Early guesses are terrible. But each time it's wrong, it nudges its internal dials — called weights — slightly toward what would have been a better answer. That nudging is backpropagation: "learn from your mistakes, backwards through the machine."
  • Bite 3 — Repeat a few million times. Wrong guess, small nudge — over millions of examples the network tunes itself until it gets things right, even on inputs it has never seen. Nobody programmed the rules; it found them in the examples.

The only difference from the kid? Scale. Modern networks hold billions of those little dials, which is how the same simple trick scales from spotting dogs to translating languages and driving cars.

Tools & Techniques

Tools Used (Disclosed)

Anthropic Claude and OpenAI ChatGPT were used as drafting and stress-testing partners, with all AI use disclosed in the original submission. The final wording, editorial judgment, and iteration decisions were my own — the AI was correct on the first attempt; making it understandable took three human-directed revisions.

Communication Strategies Applied

Storytelling — one relatable narrative carries the entire concept. Chunking — three short "bites" prevent cognitive overload. Progressive disclosure — intuition first, technical labels (weights, backpropagation) only after the idea lands. Feedback loops — iterating with the AI mirrored the very concept being explained.

Why This Artifact Matters (Value Proposition)

Artifact 1 shows I can design an AI experience; Artifact 2 shows I can select the right AI approach. Artifact 3 completes the picture: I can make AI understandable to the people who must adopt it. In enterprise automation, the limiting factor is rarely model accuracy — it is whether operations teams, risk partners, and executives genuinely understand what the system does. Accuracy doesn't create understanding; communication does.
