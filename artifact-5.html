Introduction

This artifact analyzes a significant AI incident and demonstrates the critical-thinking and ethical judgment that separate a mature AI/ML leader from a pure technologist. It speaks directly to the risk and compliance audience this portfolio targets.

Description

A written critical analysis of Amazon's experimental AI recruiting tool, developed from 2014 to 2018, which learned to downgrade female candidates because it was trained on roughly a decade of male-dominated resumes. The piece identifies three ethical issues, explains the reasoning behind each, and proposes a mitigation strategy.

Objective

Identify and critically analyze three ethical issues in a significant AI incident, then reflect on how my own thinking evolved and how that will shape my future approach to AI development.

Process

I selected the case from the AI Incident Database, summarized the key facts from primary reporting, then worked through three ethical lenses: algorithmic amplification of historical bias, opaque decision-making, and weak governance. For each I recorded why I chose it, my analytical thought process, and a concrete mitigation. I closed with a reflection on a changed assumption, that more data automatically means fairer outcomes.

Tools and Tech Used

Critical-analysis and algorithmic-bias frameworks, fairness-audit and explainability concepts, and source validation against Reuters, BBC, the ACLU, and the AI Incident Database. No model was built; the competency demonstrated is ethical reasoning over deployed machine learning.
